## What to build

The transport strip in the Timeline panel's reserved slot above the
frame ruler (web transport-strip parity, SwiftUI-native controls),
driving the 288 playback controller:

- **Play/Pause**: a toggle that clearly shows whether playback is
  running.
- **Loop**: a toggle that clearly shows whether looping is on.
- **Playhead indication** while playing — which frame is currently
  displayed, visually distinct from the Active-Frame highlight (which
  marks the edit pointer and does not move during playback).
- Controls are **disabled for a single-frame document** (the strip's
  "needs 2+ frames" convention).
- Keyboard operable on Mac hardware keyboards where the shell's existing
  shortcut plumbing reaches; controls announce their state to
  accessibility (toggle semantics, not bare buttons).

The strip stays a thin view over the controller — no timing logic of its
own.

## Acceptance criteria

- Play cycles the canvas through the frames at their authored durations;
  Pause stops it and the canvas returns to the Active Frame.
- Loop on repeats from the start; Loop off plays once and settles.
- The playhead indication tracks the displayed frame and is
  distinguishable from the active-frame highlight.
- Both toggles reflect their state visually and to accessibility; both
  are disabled with one frame.
- Touch targets ≥ 44 pt; new labels in the String Catalog (en/ko/ja);
  snapshot baselines updated (strip replaces the placeholder slot).
